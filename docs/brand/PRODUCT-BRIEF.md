# iBookit — Product Brief for brand, marketing and design work

Everything a logo, identity or marketing-site designer needs. This is what the product **does
today**, not a roadmap — §6 is the do-not-claim list. The name is settled (§7): design *for* it.
Sourced detail behind §2: `docs/Context/2026-08-08-membership-booking-market-taxonomy.md`.

---

## 1. What it is

**iBookit** runs the front desk of any business built on memberships and bookings — the roster, the
schedule, the money, and who showed up — and ships wearing **that business's own brand**, not ours.

It replaces the paper notebook, the WhatsApp group and the Excel sheet, by answering four questions
live for the owner: **who are my clients** · **what did they pay for and when does it run out** ·
**what's on the calendar and who's coming** · **who actually showed up**. A fifth — *how is the
business doing* — is the next thing being built.

## 2. Who it's for

**Not "gyms."** The dividing line isn't the industry, it's how the business sells time: iBookit is
for anyone selling **a spot in a scheduled group session, against a roster of named people who come
back**. That one shape spans industries with nothing else in common.

**Do not promise these** — they look adjacent and aren't: canchas de padel, pickleball y futbol 5
(they rent a court by the hour) · consultorios médicos, dentales, fisioterapia, salones de belleza y
veterinarias (a 1:1 book on a named practitioner) · autoescuelas y bootcamps (fixed cohorts with
tuition installments) · clubes campestres y colegios de profesionistas (dues, no attendance) ·
crioterapia y tanques de flotación (one machine per booking). **Kids' academies** are strong demand,
but the product can't yet separate the paying parent from the enrolled child — headline them once
that ships.

**The rule this sets for the brand:** the identity must be **vertical-neutral**. No dumbbells, no
barbells, no gym iconography, no fitness-bro energy. A piano school, a climbing gym and a dance
academy all have to see themselves in it.

## 3. What ships

Two web apps off one system; every business gets both.

**Staff console** (desk and phone)

- **Inicio** — today's check-ins, the week's revenue, who's due to renew
- **Clientes** — searchable roster; per-person file with plan, sessions and days left, expiry, attendance, every payment
- **Asistencia** — one-tap check-in, today or backdated, to open access or a specific class
- **Agenda** — day/week schedule; one-off or repeating sessions, per-session roster, book a client or add a walk-in
- **Vender** — sell or renew in a few taps; cash, card or transfer; issues a numbered receipt sent by WhatsApp or email
- **Cuenta** — prices, class types, coaches, message templates, the business's public-page content, monthly Excel export

**Member app** (mobile web, no install)

- The business's own public pages — home, prices, about, contact — with owner-written content
- Sign up, or activate the account the desk created via emailed invite
- **Reservar** — the week's classes with live spots-remaining; book, cancel, favorite
- **Class detail** — coach, what you'll do, what to bring, who's coming
- **Perfil** — plan, sessions left, expiry, upcoming bookings

## 4. What we sell that isn't software

1. **Use our brand.** A finished house identity ships with the product — a business with no brand
   goes live looking deliberate, having designed nothing.
2. **Or wear your own.** Colors, logo, name, app icon, receipt and member emails all swap to the
   business's identity. Two businesses already run this way; neither looks like the other.
3. **Or we make you one.** No brand yet? We design it, then the platform wears it.
4. **We move your members in — all of them, from whatever mess you have.** A handwritten notebook. A
   WhatsApp group. A ten-year-old spreadsheet with three spellings of the same name. A competitor's
   export. We take it in any state, reconcile it with the owner, and load it. Day one opens with
   their **real roster already inside**, never an empty database. A done-for-you service, not a
   file-upload button.

Onboarding is hand-done by us, per business — which is what makes #4 credible.

## 5. Market, buyer, voice

- **Market:** Mexico first, LatAm next. The product speaks **es-MX**; copy is Spanish-first.
- **Buyer:** an owner-operator — 1–15 staff, 30–600 clients. On their phone all day, price-sensitive,
  will abandon anything needing a setup weekend. No IT department behind them.
- **End user:** that owner's client, on a phone, wanting one thing — *what time is it and do I have a spot.*
- **Voice:** plain, direct, warm. No enterprise SaaS jargon, no growth-hacker energy, no
  "revolutionize your business."

## 6. Do not claim

- **No online or in-app payments.** Money is collected at the desk (cash, card terminal, transfer)
  and *recorded* — no checkout, no card entry, no recurring billing, no processor. The member app
  says so: *"los pagos se realizan directamente en tu gimnasio."*
- **No native app.** Both are web; the member app is mobile-first web — no App Store, no install.
- **No SMS or push.** WhatsApp is a prefilled message a human sends, not an automated channel.
- **No waitlist** for a full class.
- **No analytics or reports page yet** — a monthly summary card and an Excel export are all of it.
- **No self-serve signup for a business.**
- **No CFDI e-invoicing, no multi-currency.** Pesos only.
- **No staff-permissions screen, no workout tracking, no in-app chat.**

## 7. Naming — locked

- **The product is `iBookit`** — capital B, one word.
- **It lives at `ibooki.lat`.** The domain drops the final *t* on purpose: the name is **iBookit**,
  the address is **ibooki.lat**. Neither is a typo of the other; never "correct" one into the other.
- **`RED` and `Forge` are client brands** — two real businesses running on iBookit, never the
  product's name.
- The name is English in a Spanish-speaking market: the wordmark must read cleanly to someone who
  will pronounce it phonetically.
- Production still answers on the older `ibookit.lat`; the move is pending. Write for `ibooki.lat`,
  don't hard-code live URLs.
