# iBookit — Brand Strategy (Layer 1)

Phase B of `BRAND-PLAN.md`. Facts from `PRODUCT-BRIEF.md` and competitor research
(2026-08-10, sources at end). Standalone: no other doc needed to use this one.

## Positioning statement

For **owner-operators of businesses that sell spots in scheduled group sessions** — martial
arts, yoga, dance, swim, climbing, ceramics, anything with a roster of named people who come
back — who run the desk today from a notebook, a WhatsApp group and an Excel sheet, **iBookit
is the front-desk system that runs the roster, the schedule, the money and the attendance
under the business's own brand** — unlike gym software, which speaks fitness, looks like the
vendor, and makes every client business look like every other.

## Category decision

**Claimed:** front-desk software for class-and-membership businesses (es-MX handle: *el
sistema de tu recepción* — negocios de clases y membresías).

**Rejected:** "software para gimnasios" — the crowded head term. Every competitor sampled
anchors to gym/fitness; none names swim schools, ceramics or climbing as peers. The
vertical-neutral position is unclaimed.

**Trade-off accepted:** we give up the high-volume "software para gimnasios" search term and
instant category recognition. In exchange: an uncontested position that matches the product
truth (white-label, vertical-neutral) and doesn't repel the piano school. Vertical landing
pages can chase the head terms later — application concern, out of scope here.

**GTM constraint:** MX-native players publish tú-register MXN pricing ($799–$3,999/mo);
gated "request a demo" pricing reads foreign and enterprise. Positioning must survive a
published price.

## Audience

**Primary (and buyer — same person):** the owner-operator. 1–15 staff, 30–600 clients, on
their phone all day, price-sensitive, no IT department, will abandon anything needing a setup
weekend. Daily context: at the desk between sessions, takes cash, chases renewals over
WhatsApp, keeps the truth of the business in their head.

**Secondary:** their coaches and front-desk staff — users, never buyers.

**Niche breadth (sales scope):** beyond the brief's 12 genres, see
`docs/Context/2026-08-10-sales-niche-sweep.md` — 8 additional STRONG niches (tennis/circo/
parkour/chess/choral/regularización-grupal/teatro infantil …) and the capacity-1
private-lessons ruling (fixed weekly slot + same teacher = sellable PARTIAL).

**Not a brand audience:** the end member. The platform is invisible to them by design
(CP1-recorded decision); they see their gym's brand, not ours.

## Value propositions (ranked)

1. **It wears your brand, not ours.** Colors, logo, name, app icon, receipts, emails — all
   swap. Three doors, always stated together (brief §4, CP2 ruling): wear your own brand; no
   brand yet? use our finished house identity; or we design you one and the platform wears
   it. Two businesses run fully branded today; no competitor sampled makes any of this the
   headline (it's a buried feature or a paid add-on everywhere). This is the flag.
2. **Day one opens with your real roster inside.** We take the notebook, the ten-year-old
   spreadsheet, the competitor export — reconcile it with you and load it. Done-for-you, not
   a file-upload button. Competitors offer "free migration" as reassurance copy; none makes
   it the promise.
3. **Four questions, answered live:** who are my clients · what did they pay and when does it
   run out · what's on the calendar and who's coming · who actually showed up. The honest
   core utility that replaces the notebook/WhatsApp/Excel triad.

## The alternative (honest)

Not Mindbody. The real competitor is **the current system**: a paper notebook, a WhatsApp
group and an Excel sheet — free, familiar, fully offline, and working right up until "¿quién
no ha pagado?" has no answer. The pitch must respect it, not mock it: it's what the product's
own vocabulary came from.

## Brand adjectives

- **Recessive, not invisible** — steps back so the client's brand is the loudest thing in the
  room; every detail still deliberate. (CP1: this is the differentiator.)
- **Warm, not playful** — human and tú-register; no mascots, no confetti.
- **Plain, not basic** — talks like the front desk about lists, money and who showed up; the
  system underneath is not simple and doesn't need to say so.
- **Modern, not techy** — current and clean without gradients, dashboard worship or AI-speak.

plain operational language, published MXN pricing.

## Voice principles (es-MX)

1. **Habla como el mostrador, no como el software.** Use the product's own vocabulary
   (pasar lista, vigencia, por renovar, paquete).
   - Do: *"¿Quién vino hoy? Ya lo sabes."*
   - Don't: *"Optimiza la gestión de asistencia de tu centro."*
2. **Tú, siempre. Nunca usted, nunca "el usuario".**
   - Do: *"Tu lista de clientes, tal como la tienes — nosotros la cargamos."*
   - Don't: *"El administrador podrá visualizar el listado de socios."*
3. **Promete lo que hace hoy.** The do-not-claim list (brief §6) binds marketing copy too.
   - Do: *"Los pagos se registran en tu recepción: efectivo, terminal o transferencia."*
   - Don't: *"Revoluciona tu negocio con pagos inteligentes."*
