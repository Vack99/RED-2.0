# iBookit — Identity Direction (Layer 2)

Phase C of `BRAND-PLAN.md`. Inherits `01-strategy.md`; intent only — no hex, no logos
(execution is Phase C2). Standalone.

## Visual register

| Axis | Position | Why (from strategy, not taste) |
|---|---|---|
| technical ↔ approachable | approachable, just off center | Buyer has no IT dept and abandons anything needing a setup weekend; but it records money, so it can't read as a toy |
| dense ↔ airy | airy | Recessive is the differentiator; density reads as vendor presence |
| warm ↔ cool | warm | "Warm, not playful" adjective; the category defaults cool blue/teal — warmth is the visible break |
| serious ↔ playful | serious, softened by warmth | It answers "¿quién no ha pagado?" — trust surface; playful is Klasius's lane and childish is banned |

## Colour intent (no hex)

- **Neutral-dominant.** The system is mostly quiet warm neutrals — paper-like light surfaces,
  soft deep darks — with **one** restrained accent used scarcely (actions, focus, the mark).
  Accent scarcity IS the recessiveness.
- The accent must not collide with semantic states: success/green, error/red, warning exist
  in the token contract and carry meaning (vigente/vencido/por renovar). So the accent lives
  away from green, red and alarm-orange.
- Not the category's blue/teal SaaS default; not gritty athletic black/red; not neon.
- **Two expressions, one family** (plan rule 5): corporate (marketing site, docs, platform
  emails) may use the accent with slightly more confidence; the **house theme** (`base`
  module) uses the same DNA turned down further — an unconfigured client business must look
  deliberate and self-owned, never iBookit-colored.
- Light and dark both first-class (apps ship both schemes); marketing site is light-first.
- WCAG AA everywhere, AAA body text on marketing (plan rule 7).

## Typographic intent

- **One humanist sans superfamily** for display and body — warm, open apertures, nothing
  geometric-cold or condensed-athletic. Variable font, web-embeddable, open license (buyer is
  price-sensitive; so are we).
- Must carry full es-MX orthography cleanly: á é í ó ú ü ñ ¿ ¡ — display cuts included.
- **Tabular numerals required** — the product is rosters, prices, vigencias, numbered
  receipts.
- The wordmark's typographic treatment must keep "iBookit" legible to a phonetic Spanish
  reader (brief §7): the capital B is the seam — never let i/B blur into "ibookit"/"IBookit".
- Mono: not a brand need. Only if a surface demands it later (receipts already have their own
  constraints).

## Iconography & imagery (brand assets, not UI)

- **The three-businesses test:** every icon set, illustration or photo direction must work
  equally for a piano school, a climbing gym and a dance academy (and now circo, chess,
  regularización — see the niche sweep). Any vertical prop appears only in equal-weight sets,
  never solo as "the" image.
- Iconography: simple geometric line work derived from the product's real nouns — roster,
  calendar, receipt, check-in — not from any vertical's equipment.
- Imagery: real people at their craft and at the desk, warm light, documentary not
  aspirational; never athletic stock, never dashboard-screenshot heroes.
- The client's brand is the protagonist in any product imagery — show RED's red and Forge's
  look, not ours (the white-label story told visually).

|---|---|---|
| Notion | Calm warm-neutral surfaces; the tool recedes behind the user's content | Its illustration quirk |
| Shopify | Masterbrand architecture: merchants' brands front, platform brand disciplined behind | Its scale/enterprise register |

| WhatsApp Business | The zero-learning-curve familiarity MX owners already trust | Its consumer green everywhere |

## Anti-patterns (must not resemble)

- Gritty athletic (Wodify): chalk, barbells, black/red aggression — bans the biggest genre
  cliché.
- Enterprise wellness SaaS (Mindbody/Glofox): corporate blue, stock studios, gated-quote
  energy.
- Emoji-casual (Klasius): the local minimal player; we stay minimal but warm-serious, not
  chatty.
- AI-startup slop: purple gradients, glassmorphism, orbiting particles.
- Any big-impact identity: loud supergraphics, oversized wordmarks, brand-first layouts
  (CP1: we must never get in the way of a client's business).
