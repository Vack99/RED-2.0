Scope: 13 class-booking platforms. Sources are official help-center/knowledge-base articles unless
marked **[MARKETING]** (vendor blog/marketing page — informative but not authoritative) or
**[SECONDARY]** (third-party summary, not the vendor). "No primary source found" is stated explicitly
per cell where a reasonable search did not surface an official doc, per task instructions — it is not
omitted.

Web search access could fetch full page text for some URLs; many Zendesk/Intercom/Salesforce help
sites return HTTP 403 to the fetch tool, so several claims are sourced from search-engine snippets of

- Several platforms (Mariana Tek, Momence, Arketa) explicitly support studio-configurable variants of what happens to the credit on cancel/no-show, but the *initial* deduction-at-booking behavior itself is not something studios toggle — only its reversal is configurable.

## What the sources converge on

Ranked by breadth of platform/source support (most-supported first):

1. **Credit deduction happens at booking time by default, not at check-in or class start.** Directly stated or clearly implied for 11 of 13 platforms researched (ClassPass, Glofox, TeamUp, Momence, WellnessLiving, Arketa, Mariana Tek, Wodify, bsport, Eversports, and Mindbody for ClassPass-mediated bookings). Punchpass is the sole clearly-documented exception (deducts at attendance-taking). Zen Planner's default could not be confirmed.
2. **The cancellation window is studio/business-configurable everywhere it's documented**, with a loose default clustering around 12–24 hours where any default figure is stated at all (ClassPass ~12h, WellnessLiving 24h default, Punchpass "24 hours is pretty standard," Mindbody/Glofox/TeamUp/Momence/Arketa/Mariana Tek/Wodify all business-set with no fixed platform default). Supported by 8+ platforms.
3. **No-show is treated as a materially worse outcome than a same-window late-cancel** wherever the two are distinguished — via a bigger fee (ClassPass), distinct penalty statuses (Mariana Tek's "penalty cancel" vs. "penalty no-show"), or separate configuration knobs (WellnessLiving, Wodify, Arketa, TeamUp's Penalty Systems). Supported by 6 platforms' docs.
4. **Member-facing visibility of the forfeiture is the exception, not the rule, in what vendors document.** Only 3 of 13 platforms have any documented visibility mechanic — ClassPass (default, multi-channel: page/popup/email), Mariana Tek (default, but passive — a reservation-history record, not a push notification), TeamUp (opt-in email trigger). The other 10 platforms' help docs describe the deduction/penalty mechanic in detail but are silent on whether/how the member is shown it. This silence itself is the most consistent single pattern across all 13 platforms' documentation.
5. **Where a "staff never took roll" auto-resolution exists, it is time-boxed and automatic, but there is no converged industry-standard duration.** 5 of 13 platforms document an explicit auto-no-show mechanism (Mindbody 10 min [marketing-sourced], Wodify 1 hour, Momence 24 hours, Arketa ~next business day via daily batch, Mariana Tek "no-show window" of unstated length) — a 100x+ spread between the shortest and longest documented windows. Roughly half the platforms researched (7 of 13) have no primary source describing this edge case at all, despite it being a routine operational failure mode.
6. **No platform was found to document a "credits held by upcoming bookings vs. free to book" dual-balance display.** The two most concrete UI descriptions found (WellnessLiving's remaining/total bar, Wodify's "used X of Y" check-in message) are both remaining-vs-total, not held-vs-free — consistent with finding #1: because credit is spent at booking, there is structurally nothing left to "hold" separately.
7. **Credit forfeiture has produced real external friction for at least one platform, escalating past UX complaints into litigation.** Two separate class actions against ClassPass (2023, 2025) allege undisclosed or unfairly-structured credit loss; the 2025 suit explicitly characterizes the design as exploiting consumer loss-aversion. This is the strongest available prior-art evidence that silent credit forfeiture is a genuine trust/liability risk, not merely a hypothetical concern — though it is evidenced by a single platform's litigation history rather than a cross-platform pattern.
