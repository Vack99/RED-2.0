# Legal papering in gym/class-booking SaaS: what the niche actually does

## Direct answer

The ruled iBookit model **matches the niche standard on every structural axis that could be verified, and appears to exceed it on one (on thin evidence)**. One platform ToS with data-processing terms incorporated by reference (no separately negotiated DPA) is the dominant pattern — Mindbody, Glofox, Wodify and Gymdesk all do this; only TeamUp makes the DPA a separate request-and-sign document. Explicit acceptance at self-serve signup is confirmed at the two vendors whose signup forms were actually fetched (TeamUp: two checkboxes; **Gymdesk: one combined checkbox covering ToS + privacy + DPA** — a direct precedent for the ruled model); Trainerize uses click-through tied to the "Create My Account" button rather than a verified checkbox (*inferred*). Continued use is the amendment-acceptance mechanic at every vendor whose clause was read, and **no vendor requires blanket re-acceptance** — with one narrow exception: Wodify reserves affirmative consent for arbitration/fee changes "where required by law." **Zero documented instances of any vendor in this niche blocking an already-logged-in customer behind a legal wall were found** (bounded negative search evidence, *unverified*). Contracting-entity thinness is not an outlier: Gymdesk, arguably the closest peer, contracts as "Martial Arts on Rails, LLC, d.b.a. Gymdesk" out of an Austin suite address.

The one axis where iBookit may **exceed** the verified niche: a platform-generated per-gym aviso naming the GYM as responsable. Among the three MX/LatAm vendors examined, no vendor was shown to do this — but that conclusion is the subagent's own *inferred* synthesis, and one of its three legs (Fitco's platform-level policy) could not be fetched at all. See contradictions.

## 2. Acceptance mechanics and update notice

**Signup acceptance.** Directly fetched signup forms: TeamUp shows two checkboxes — "I agree to the Privacy Policy" and "I agree to the Terms of Service" (goteamup.com/signup/, verified); **Gymdesk shows one combined checkbox: "I agree to the terms of service, privacy policy and data processing agreement"** (gymdesk.com/register, verified). Trainerize uses click-through tied to the button — "By clicking Create My Account and signing up, you agree to the Terms Of Services and Privacy Policy" (trainerize.com/free-trial/, *inferred* — no checkbox affordance confirmed). Mindbody and Glofox are **demo/sales-gated** with no self-serve business signup found (mindbodyonline.com/business/pricing; glofox.com/demo/ — both *inferred*). Wodify's business self-serve path is ambiguous; Virtuagym's is genuinely contradictory across snapshots and unresolved. In MX, Fitpass confirms the checkbox pattern in its own text ("aceptarlo marcando la casilla en el formulario de registro") though it is a consumer marketplace, not gym-facing SaaS.

**Update mechanics — continued use is the acceptance mechanic everywhere the clause was read; no vendor requires blanket re-acceptance:**
- Mindbody: "Any changes we make will become effective when we post a modified version… If we make any material changes… we'll also notify you within the Software Service or by sending you an email… If you continue using the Services after any changes, it means you have accepted them." **No fixed advance-notice day count found.** (mindbodyonline.com/company/legal/terms-of-service)
- Glofox: effective at "the earlier of (a) your continued use of Purchased Services, or (b) 30 days from posting." Note this is an **outer effectiveness bound, not a 30-day advance-notice guarantee** — continued use makes the change effective immediately. (glofox.com/legal/terms-of-use/)
- Wodify: "Material changes will take effect no sooner than thirty (30) days after notice… By continuing to use the Wodify Apps… you agree that you will be subject to the modified Terms." **Partial exception:** "changes to arbitration or fee terms apply prospectively and, where required by law, only with your affirmative consent." (wodify.com/terms-of-service/)
- TeamUp: "at least seven (7) days notice before the changes take effect, during which period of time you may reject the changes by terminating your account." (goteamup.com/company/terms/)
- Trainerize: 30 days US/global; **15 days UK/EU with penalty-free termination**; continued use = acceptance. (trainerize.com/businessTerms/)
- Gymdesk: "effective upon posting," **no advance-notice period stated at all.** (gymdesk.com/page/terms)
- Fitpass: two-tier — ordinary changes reserved "sin previo aviso," but "En caso de modificaciones sustanciales… FITPASS notificará a los Usuarios mediante correo electrónico registrado." (fitpass.mx/terminosycondiciones, *inferred*)
- Boxmagic: email to the address on file, but the clause is scoped to pricing/plan changes only. (help.boxmagicapp.com/es/terminos-y-condiciones, *unverified*)
- Fitco: the only amendment clause surfaced is pricing-scoped (30 days' notice for annual tariff adjustments); no general amendment clause was found. (*inferred*)

**Notice channel norm:** email to the account owner, sometimes plus in-app notice (Mindbody). Mindbody does maintain a dedicated support article titled "Updates to MINDBODY Terms of Service," but the page returned a loading/error state and its content could not be read — so no vendor's public legal-changelog page was actually *retrieved*, which is a search gap rather than evidence none exists.

**Stated notice periods are NOT uniform.** Only Wodify (30 days, material changes) and Trainerize (30 days US / 15 days UK-EU) commit to an advance-notice period in the text read. Gymdesk states none, Mindbody states no day count, TeamUp states 7 days, Fitpass reserves the right to change ordinary terms without prior notice, and Glofox's "30 days" is an effectiveness backstop rather than notice. There is no majority 30-day norm to fall short of.

**THE KEY QUESTION — legal walls for logged-in customers.** *Unverified negative evidence:* targeted searches across Mindbody, Glofox, Wodify, TeamUp and Gymdesk returned **zero documented instances** of a mandatory terms-re-acceptance modal blocking a logged-in business customer. A general query confirmed the pattern exists in software broadly (iCloud's forced re-accept modal) but produced **zero gym-SaaS hits**, and no complaint threads of any kind surfaced in the niche. This is bounded search evidence, not proof of absence — no vendor product was inspected behind a login, and Virtuagym, Trainerize and all MX/LatAm vendors were never given a wall-targeted query at all.

**Verdict on axis 2: MATCHES.** Checkbox-at-signup plus continued-use-for-updates plus no wall is exactly what the niche does. Naming a notice period (e.g. 30 days for material changes) would put iBookit alongside Wodify and Trainerize, the two clearest peers on this point — but it is an optional upgrade, not a gap against a majority line.

## 3. Member-facing privacy — where iBookit may exceed the niche

**This axis is the weakest-evidenced part of the report: the subagent assigned to member-facing privacy architecture across all ten vendors returned a placeholder stub with no real findings.** Everything below is what other agents surfaced incidentally.

What was found:
- **No verified vendor was shown to generate a per-gym aviso naming the gym as responsable.** The claim that across Fitco, Fitpass and Boxmagic "none explicitly names the GYM/BOX/STUDIO as the responsable/controller of member personal data" is the subagent's own ***inferred*** synthesis across three documents, one of which it could not fetch.
- Fitpass publishes a real Mexican **"Aviso de Privacidad"** naming **FITPASS** as responsable, with studios as downstream recipients: the user "autoriza a FITPASS a hacer uso de dicha información… incluyendo compartir esta información con los studios que forman parte de la red FITPASS." (politicadeprivacidad.fitpass.mx/, verified)
- Boxmagic: "Los datos personales serán incorporados a una base de datos automatizada de la cual BoxMagic es responsable" — and its privacy doc is a Chilean "Política de Privacidad" under **Ley 19.628**, with **zero mention of Mexico's LFPDPPP**, despite marketing to Mexican boxes. (help.boxmagicapp.com/es/política-de-privacidad, verified)
- Fitco cuts both ways and should not be counted cleanly on either side: its ToS puts responsibility for member data **on the gym** ("el Negocio [es] el único responsable del manejo de los mismos"; "El Negocio será responsable en exclusiva del incumplimiento…", *inferred*), while its platform-level policy reportedly declares "FITCO actúa como responsable y encargada del tratamiento" of the FITCO user database — ***unverified***, direct fetch returned HTTP 403, known only via a search-engine paraphrase.
- Wodify has the clearest controller/processor framing found in any Anglo vendor's ToS: "Where your Organization (e.g., your gym or employer) is the data controller, Wodify acts as a data processor… in other contexts… Wodify may act as an independent controller." (wodify.com/terms-of-service/) — but no evidence of a generated per-gym notice.
- Glofox has an in-product e-agreements/waiver flow for **end members** (support.glofox.com), a gym-configurable document surface, but it was not verified as a privacy-notice generator.

**Verdict on axis 3: LIKELY EXCEEDS — on evidence too thin to bank.** Nothing found shows a competitor generating a per-gym aviso with the gym as responsable and the gym typing its razón social/domicilio/ARCO contact in-app. The MX/LatAm players tend structurally the *opposite* way (platform as controller). But the assigned sweep never ran, Fitco's platform policy is unfetched, and Fitco's per-establishment PDFs are unread. Treat this as a plausible differentiator and a defensible LFPDPPP posture, not an established fact about the market.
