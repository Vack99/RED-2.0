**Two different endpoints, not one contradictory number** — confirmed by fetching Supabase's own two docs pages **today (2026-07-28)**:

|---|---|---|---|---|

| `/auth/v1/otp` | (same endpoint) | **Documented as 360/hour** on the *other* page ("this has been updated to 2 emails per hour… `/auth/v1/otp` endpoint defaults to 360 OTPs per hour") | not restated | supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/going-into-prod |

**The 30-vs-360 conflict is real, current, and inside Supabase's own two pages simultaneously** — I independently reproduced it today by fetching both URLs, matching the prior audit's flag (`docs/Context/2026-07-22-invite-mail-capacity-audit.md:83`, listed as "Unverified — do not design around these"). It is a genuine unresolved Supabase documentation defect.

**But it does not gate RED's email ceiling.** RED's fresh-provision activation rail calls `admin.auth.admin.createUser({email_confirm:true})` then `admin.auth.admin.generateLink({type:"recovery"})` — both **service-role Admin API calls**, confirmed at `supabase/functions/activar-cuenta/index.ts:92-108`. Neither touches the public `/auth/v1/otp` endpoint or queues built-in GoTrue mail (matches the prior audit's "activation costs zero extra mail," now confirmed at file:line by me). The number that actually gates RED's mail is the **`email_sent` bucket** — documented default 2/hour, raised via custom SMTP, and **live-set to 50/hour and live-tested with a real 429** per `hitl-75-send-email-hook.md:66-70`. The `/auth/v1/otp` 30-vs-360 conflict only becomes load-bearing if a future feature adds a magic-link/passwordless flow — worth a one-line note for whoever builds that, not a live risk today.

**Does custom SMTP / the Send Email Hook LIFT the project-wide limit?** No — resolved, and already live-tested in-repo (not just theorized): `hitl-75-send-email-hook.md:66-70`, §g, dated 2026-07-10, records an **actual observed `429 over_email_send_rate_limit`** with the hook enabled, proving GoTrue enforces `email_sent` *before* invoking the hook. Custom SMTP only makes the number **editable** (Supabase's own rate-limits page: "You can only change this with a custom SMTP setup") — it does not remove the gate. The prior audit's own doc (`2026-07-22-invite-mail-capacity-audit.md:79`, `email-infrastructure-investigation.md:61`) filed this as "UNVERIFIED — do not design around these," but the repo's OWN §g entry from three sessions earlier already answers it with a live test. **This is a repo-internal inconsistency worth fixing**: two docs in the same repo, one saying "verified live" and a later one saying "unverified," about the same fact. I'm resolving it in the direction of the live test (§g) since it's a reproducible observation, not a theory.

**Supabase's maximum settable `email_sent` value:** still genuinely unpublished — neither of my two fetches today surfaced one. Repo's "UNVERIFIED" flag stands.

## (e) The compute — gyms-per-day onboarding ceiling and steady-state volume at 150–300 members/gym

The prior repo audit (`2026-07-22-invite-mail-capacity-audit.md:50`) built its volume ratio off **RED's real roster (40 members)**: `40 renewals + 4 new-join + 1.2 resets ≈ 45/month` → **1.125 emails/member/month**. That ratio (monthly-renewing membership, universal email coverage) is the only measured ratio in the repo; I reuse it but rescale to the task's stated **150–300 members/gym**, not RED's much smaller real numbers — this is the correction Workflow 1's flat "~19 gyms" figure needed and didn't get.

**Per-gym monthly volume:**
- 150 members × 1.125 = **169 emails/month/gym ≈ 5.6/day**
- 300 members × 1.125 = **338 emails/month/gym ≈ 11.3/day**

**Free tier (3,000/mo, 100/day, both fetched today) — steady-state gym ceiling** (daily and monthly caps coincide here because 100/day × 30 = 3,000/mo, and the model spreads volume evenly):
- 3,000 ÷ 169 ≈ **17.8 gyms** (150-member average)
- 3,000 ÷ 338 ≈ **8.9 gyms** (300-member average)

**→ Steady-state Free-tier ceiling at the task's real target size: ~9–18 gyms**, not the "~19" a 150-member-only calc gives and not the "~66" the prior audit computed off RED's 40-member roster. The task's own 150–300 member spec is 3.75–7.5× larger than RED's real roster, which pulls this ceiling down proportionally. **At the low end of the range (300-member gyms), the ceiling is worse than Workflow 1 claimed, not better.**

**Onboarding-day ceiling (bursty, not steady-state):** the repo confirms **no bulk-send path exists anywhere** — one invite = one operator click (`docs/superpowers/handoffs/2026-07-22-email-infrastructure-investigation.md:9, 40-43`; I did not re-derive this, it's inherited from the prior audit's code search, not re-verified by me this session). A 150–300-member roster therefore can't be sent in one API call, but a front-desk blitz entering members over a handful of days at a realistic ~30–50 invites/day/gym is exactly the shape that breaks the shared 100/day cap: **2–3 gyms onboarding concurrently at that pace, on top of already-onboarded gyms' background renewal/reset traffic, exhausts the entire platform's daily budget** — worse than "1 gym/day" if you read that as strictly sequential, but the same order of magnitude as Workflow 1's claim.

**Steady-state monthly volume at full 3,000-gym target** (this is what determines the eventual paid-plan requirement, not a ceiling question):
- 3,000 gyms × 169–338/month = **507,000–1,014,000 emails/month** (≈16,900–33,800/day).
- Against fetched tiers: **Free breaks at ~9–18 gyms** (above); **Pro $20 (50k/mo) covers ~148–296 gyms**; **Pro $35 (100k/mo) covers ~296–591 gyms**; the full 3,000-gym target needs a **Scale tier** in the $90–$1,150/mo band depending on exact volume in the 500k–1M/mo range (resend.com/pricing, fetched today).
- **Email cost is not a business-model risk at any tier fetched.** Even the top of that band ($1,150/mo) is trivial against the stated 300–1,500 MXN/gym/month price point (3,000 gyms even at the low end ≈ 900,000 MXN/month ≈ tens of thousands of USD/month revenue). The risk is entirely **capacity-cap + operational**, never **price**.

**Sensitivity check (falsification, rule 3):** the 1.125-emails/member/month ratio assumes every member has an email on file and every membership renews monthly. Live repo data says otherwise — `email-infrastructure-investigation.md:69`: only **34/86 clientes (≈40%) had an email** as of 2026-07-22 (I did not re-query live prod for a fresher count this session — my mandate is read-only and this number is a few days old, but it's the only measured figure in the repo). If real email-coverage stays near 40% at scale, effective volume drops to ~0.45/member/month, and the steady-state Free ceiling rises to **~22–45 gyms** instead of 9–18. **This is the single biggest lever on the number** — it does not change the ranking (email is still first, by a wide margin, either way) but it roughly doubles the gym-count at which it bites. I flag it as unresolved rather than picking a side: whoever owns this number should decide whether to model against 100% coverage (conservative, current repo ratio) or ~40% (measured, but from a 4-gym, non-representative sample).

## Forced ranking — 5 worst things about the email ceiling, worst first

1. **The bounce/complaint budget is account-wide, penalty is undisclosed instant suspension, and it can be tripped at gym #1, today** — independent of gym count. Confirmed today: Resend's own policy speaks only of "your account" with no domain carve-out (resend.com/legal/acceptable-use), and the same API key is both the invite-sending key and the Supabase SMTP password (`hitl-72-resend-live.md:78`), so a suspension stops invites, receipts, AND all auth mail for every gym simultaneously. **Breaks at:** one bad address in a small-enough batch — the prior audit measured a lifetime denominator of 28 stamped invites ever, where one hard bounce in an 18-address batch is already 5.6%, above the 4% line. This is the only ceiling on this list that isn't gated by gym count at all.
2. **The Free-tier 100/day + 3,000/mo caps are account-wide across every tenant, and at the task's actual 150–300-member target they bind at ~9–18 gyms in pure steady state** (recomputed above from the repo's own measured per-member ratio) — materially earlier than the ~66-gym figure the prior audit got by using RED's much smaller real roster, and roughly matching-to-worse than Workflow 1's flat "~19." **Breaks at:** the 9th–18th gym signs, or sooner if the mix skews toward 300-member gyms.
3. **Every non-2xx from Resend is a silent, unretried, unclassified drop** — `invitaciones.ts:68` stringifies the HTTP status but never reads `retry-after`/`ratelimit-reset`/`x-resend-daily-quota`, so a hard quota wall and a single bad address produce byte-for-byte the same failure mode. At today's ~1–4 emails/day this is "a human notices the chip and re-clicks." At the volumes ceiling #2 implies, it becomes invisible platform-wide failure with no queue, no outbox, no alerting anywhere in the repo to surface it.
4. **Supabase's own two documentation pages disagree about a live number on their platform** (`/auth/v1/otp`: 30/hr vs 360/hr, both re-fetched today, both current) — a vendor-side defect RED cannot fix. It does not gate RED's critical path today (that's the separately-confirmed 50/hr `email_sent` bucket), but it means the ceiling for any future magic-link feature is genuinely unknowable from Supabase's own docs, and the repo's own audit trail on the adjacent "does custom SMTP lift the limit" question already contradicts itself across two dated docs (§g's live 429 test vs. a later doc re-flagging the same question "unverified").
5. **The fix is a $20/mo billing toggle, zero code — which makes this a negligence risk, not an architecture risk.** Nothing in the repo meters or alerts on Resend quota consumption (confirmed at `invitaciones.ts:68`, headers discarded), so the trigger to upgrade depends entirely on someone remembering to check before the 9th–18th gym, not on any system surfacing it. Compare to the database ceiling (~167 gyms per Workflow 1's `mi_membresia` claim, which this mandate did not re-derive) — that one needs an index migration and possibly a compute-tier decision; this one needs a click in a billing dashboard. Easiest ceiling in the whole stack to remove, and the one most likely to be forgotten because it looks trivial.

## Verdict

**Yes — email is confirmed as the first ceiling in the stack, by roughly an order of magnitude, but it is also the cheapest one to remove.** The tightest number is not a gym-count at all: the account-wide bounce/complaint budget (Resend, confirmed today: 4% / 0.08%, "shutdown without warning," no domain isolation) can fail at gym #1 regardless of scale. The next-tightest is a real gym-count ceiling: **Resend Free's shared 100/day + 3,000/mo caps bind at ~9–18 gyms** once the model uses the task's actual 150–300-member target instead of RED's much smaller real roster (~9 at the pessimistic 300-member end, ~18 at the optimistic 150-member end, roughly doubling to ~22–45 if the live ~40% email-coverage rate holds at scale instead of the repo's 100%-coverage ratio). Either bound is far below the earliest database ceiling this mandate did not re-derive but was handed as ~167 gyms (Workflow 1's `mi_membresia` claim) — so the ranking holds even under the most generous email-side assumptions.

**What would make this wrong:** (a) the Resend account is already on Pro, not Free — unverifiable by me this session, owner must check billing directly; if so, ceilings 2/3/5 above evaporate and only ceiling 1 (bounce/complaint) and ceiling 4 (vendor doc contradiction, dormant) remain live. (b) email coverage at scale is much lower than the repo's 100%-coverage ratio assumes (the one live data point — 40% — already suggests this), which would roughly double every gym-count above without changing the ranking. (c) the ~167-gym DB ceiling this verdict is compared against is itself wrong — I did not re-derive it and flag that as inherited, not confirmed.

**Exit trigger:** upgrade Resend Free → Pro ($20/mo, zero code, `resend.com/pricing`) before the 9th gym signs a contract (conservative bound using the 300-member end of the target range), and independently, before ANY single onboarding event could plausibly generate >80 emails in one day (the account-wide daily cap headroom below 100). Neither trigger removes ceiling 1 (bounce/complaint) — that needs address-hygiene process (staged sends, a bounce webhook → suppression list), not a billing change, and it is live today regardless of plan.
